# Surveying the Past

A few days ago, I reread one of my older pieces, [A Certain Level of Arrogance](/writing/certain-level/). My aim with the piece was to criticize two mindsets I encountered in tech-adjacent fields. First, those who carry undue confidence into areas where they lack exposure. Second, those who prioritize embellishment over skill acquisition.

I still believe in the overall message, and I think the blog communicates it decently. However, I dislike the presentation. For one, my discussion in the section "Difficulty Imbalance" is pretty hypocritical. To illustrate

> In general, it is true, though not universally true, that the skills of a tech major are relatively more difficult to learn than the skills of other non-tech majors, particularly non-STEM fields.

In other words, I am conceding that *there exists* a hierarchy between STEM and non-STEM skills. Without citing any reasoning. A perfect display of the very arrogance I'm criticizing!

My main dislike, however, is how *certain* and *heavy-handed* I am with my criticism. Consider the following passage

> Truth of the matter is that many of these people who neglect non-tech skills also have little regard for actually learning. Instead, the focus is to pick and choose the things that maximize results. While a tech degree teaches more than using technology, that's effectively what these people get out of it.

These are pretty sweeping generalizations to make with little supporting evidence. In-between the lines there may be a nugget of truth, yet the framing is condescending. Moreover, the conclusions are *very* premature. What if I misread their intentions? My critiques are also a bit misguided: even though I come off as criticizing *people*, I really mean to criticize *mindsets*.

Seeing such heavy-handedness left a sour taste in my mouth. Is it isolated to this one blog, or do I write like that in general?

Lo and behold, consider another passage from [One Down, Four To Go](/writing/one-down/), published only three months ago.

> Having previously completed the UCSB equivalent of STATS 205, PSTAT 215A, I petitioned for a waiver. Everyone in the statistics department approved my waiver, notably the professor of 205 and the vice chair of the department. They agreed that I demonstrated knowledge of the material, and that I would gain nothing from the course. Unfortunately, the unqualified bureaucrats outside the department disagreed... Talk about utter incompetency.

The frustrations I articulate are ones I still hold today. Nevertheless, I am quick to hold those bureaucrats{{ footnote() }} as incompetent at their jobs. Over one single encounter.

My frustration is also misdirected. I am mad with the policy set by the school, not those enforcing it. In a similar vein, I imply that circumventing it should be an obvious possibility, whose explanation is self-evident. This is far from the truth, for my scenario is the exception, not the norm.

These cases are not cherry-picked. Finding similar examples across my writing was fairly straightforward. More broadly, this reveals a fairly deep character flaw of mine: allowing frustration to hasten my conclusions{{ footnote() }}.

# Confronting Reality

My impression was to go back through my blogs and entirely rewrite them. Of course, it didn't take long for me to realize how terrible of an idea this is.

First, I make it no secret how much I value honesty and transparency. I feel that going back and changing entire blogs to sound better comes off as performative. Allowing myself to err is a clear demonstration that I avoid renegading my own principles.

Second, and most importantly, I think doing so is counterproductive. I am a growing person whose maturity, values, and even stylistic choices all change with time. There are probably elements of this blog I will disagree with ten years from now. Polishing the writing over and over again is just daunting, and frankly, stupid.

There's also a much deeper side to leaving the writing unchanged. Each blog is a snapshot into my character at that particular moment in time. Rewriting them is destroying an artifact of the person that wrote it. I certainly don't want my future self to desecrate the version of me writing this. These blogs collectively form a chronology into my evolution as a person. Removing the essence of this chronology is, in my opinion, prioritizing perfection over what makes me human.

Of course, I think it is also wrong to do nothing. I find many of my examples insulting, necessitating a change. And at the end of the day, I can freely disown my opinions without apology nor explanation to anyone. Part of evolving is discarding parts of my character I no longer agree with, which may include shelving them entirely. Putting all my flaws on display is more about insecure perfectionism than it is enlightening penance. I also question why anyone is entitled to an apology warranting penance for something as minor as harsh language.

Ultimately, I went through and either edited particularly strong language, or added a disclaimer. Restricting myself between the extremes of doing nothing versus doing everything is silly. After all, I decide what's on this website, and am accountable only to myself.

# My Own Character

The underlying subject of this blog isn't poor writing choices. Rather, it's a character flaw of mine. I tend to let frustration, sometimes pride, hasten my conclusions. Ironically, I criticized Ronald Fisher for doing the same in [my most recent blog](/writing/intelligence-upper-bound/).

There is no shame in admitting this. Everyone has flaws, even the mightiest moral figures. Holding myself to perfection is unrealistic. Rewriting my past is performative. Instead, I feel the most appropriate course of action is to simply correct as needed.

At the end of the day, I am at the helm of myself. How I decide to approach problems is entirely my decision. I write this blog not to announce anything, but because I find the matter interesting and worthy of discussion. Engaging in dialogue is also one of the most productive ways for me to perform introspection. Sometimes, this takes the form of personal journals, private conversations, or public blogs. I decide what goes where :)
